Proposal · 9 October 2026 · for Vicky

Projects & Finance, made simple

You said Projects and Finance feel super complex, unintuitive and heavy. Five studies measured why. Each one walked the real app as a designer on site, an owner on Monday and an admin paying vendors, and the fifth compared how Houzz Pro, Buildertrend, Procore, Studio Designer, Monograph, Xero and eight others organise the same jobs. Nothing is wrong with the features. The heaviness comes from how they're organised. Below are the seven root causes, one idea that fixes them, concept screens drawn with the demo studio's real records, and the decisions only you can make. Every feature stays.

Concepts are drawn in the product's own look with Benjamin Han's project (P-2026-002) and Demo Studio's finance records. Full findings are in design-source/context/simplify-projects-finance/.

02 · Why it feels heavy

Seven root causes, all fixable without removing anything

1

"What needs you" lives in seven places, and they disagree

The header's button, the AI brief, Project stages, MY TASKS, six tab counts, Latest updates and the Money card each hold a piece. Some things that need someone appear in none of them: a PO to approve, a bill to pay, a client's payment to confirm, an unread client message.

Ryan's header says Post an update while 2 milestones are overdue and a payment waits to be confirmed.

2

Tabs follow record types, not jobs

Talking with the client spans Updates, Messages, Tickets, Questionnaire, Choices and Floor plan comments, so a site photo has 5 possible homes. Finance splits one vendor's money across Purchase orders, Bills, Payments out and Vendors.

Benchmark: Buildertrend groups ~20 job tools under 5 menus; Xero sorts bills by status, not by document type.

3

The same fact, again and again

Benjamin's overdue Mid-Project invoice appears 5 times on the Overview and 11 times on the Money tab. One invoice is spread over five tables: Payment schedule, Invoices sent, Other invoices, Payments and Credit notes.

Benjamin's Money tab prints 57 money figures; only 17 are different.

4

Red means five different things

The studio's own late work, the client's lateness, a vendor's reply, a client's "no" and a stall all use one red warning row. Ryan shows 8 red rows; the owner can't tell whose move it is.

Procore's project home is built on "ball in court": every open item says whose move it is.

5

Money is two silos stacked on one long page

Money in runs first; costs start 1,900px down (5.3 phone screens). Profit, which combines both, is buried inside Costs and shows the whole contract before any costs exist (Marcus: "Profit S$48,600"). Finance and Reports disagree on overdue (S$1,400 vs S$15,477) and margin (63.2% vs 87.7%).

Praised tools hang money on one spine: Monograph's phase timeline, JobTread's job budget.

6

The phone is a shrunk desktop

On site there's no Create strip, so logging a call takes 6–7 presses. The first actionable row sits below the fold, and the Money tab is 7,222px long. Logging a claim asks for the receipt last.

Buildertrend's mobile opens on a job summary with one capture button; a receipt goes into a Cost Inbox to file later.

7

The steps between features are left to memory

Each feature works well on its own, but the moves between them are separate flows. Getting a won job ready asks for the start date in 3 places. Seven steps that always follow are prompted by nothing: send the deposit; turn on "ask the client to confirm" before sharing; start the milestone's wait when a choice is shared; draft the VO after a priced pick; tell the client about a hold or a handover; the start and done prompts, which vanish on reload; Remind, which lives only in the Invite to Portal drawer.

Benjamin's header says Share changes while his payment is 7 days overdue: the button's order ignores urgency, and a ready invoice hides Share.

03 · The idea

Every page answers three questions, in this order

This is what the Leads module already does well and what the best tools converged on. The client's portal already opens on "What needs you". The studio's side gets the same twin.

1 · Act

What needs me?

One ranked list, each row with its one button. The header's filled button is simply the top row, so the two can never disagree.

2 · Plan

What's next?

What's coming this week and next, and what we're waiting on the client for, with Remind on the row.

3 · Glance

Where does it stand?

A few honest numbers, each shown once, each opening its list. Profit appears only when it means something.

  • One home per job. Tools that are one job seen from two sides are grouped. Every current address keeps working.
  • Each fact once. Other places link to it rather than repeating it.
  • One list, several views. One Money out list with a kind switch, not four tabs; one Billing list with the payment stages as its spine.
  • Red means it's late and it's ours. The client's lateness is neutral, with Remind. A vendor's reply is neutral, with Open.
  • The role shapes the page by its data. A designer sees what the server already hands them; no toggles.
  • The phone gets its own job. Today on site: what needs me, Create in reach, the receipt first.
  • Nothing removed. Every capability keeps a home (section 10), every rule and approval stays, and Undo stays on every step.
  • One sheet per moment. Steps that always happen together become default-on ticks in one sheet, each keeping its own approval rule, and the sheet ends with one WhatsApp draft.
  • Automatic only where nothing reaches the client without a press. Drafts, waits, rows and prompts happen by themselves. Sends never do.
  • House pieces only. The lead's frame, MY TASKS, the LEAD STAGES row, the listing's table, Chips, the cost panel, the house Dialog. No new UI kit.

05 · The project's life, as moments

Steps that always go together become one sheet

A real-world moment such as "carpentry's done" is four flows in the app today, on five screens, with three WhatsApp drafts to the same client and two traps along the way. Untick a step to skip it, and each step stays reachable on its own tab. The sheet ends with one sentence, one Undo and one WhatsApp draft.

09 · Evidence

What the best tools do, and what it means for us

From their help docs, product tours and reviews. The leaders don't have fewer modules than OpenMaison. They group them, hide what a job doesn't use, open on what needs you and push digests. Full sources are in R-benchmark.md.

Buildertrend · Procore

Land on what needs me

Buildertrend rebuilt its landing page because people "would begin there but quickly navigate to another page". Procore's project home lists open items by whose move it is.

Buildertrend · Houzz Pro

Many tools, few headings

About 20 job tools under 5 menus. Houzz Pro lets a studio switch off tiles it doesn't use. Our 14 → 7.

Monograph · JobTread · CoConstruct

Money on one spine

The phase timeline, the job budget or the single estimate. Other documents are converted from it, not retyped. Our payment stages become Billing's spine.

Xero · QuickBooks

Browse by status, not by document type

Xero's bills: Draft · Awaiting approval · Awaiting payment · Paid, with dashboard links landing on Awaiting payment. Our To do and Money out.

Buildertrend · QuickBooks

Costs come in through an inbox

Capture a receipt first, file it later: Buildertrend's Cost Inbox, QuickBooks' "For review". Our receipt-first claim on a phone.

CoConstruct · Houzz Pro

The client sees the studio's view, filtered

"Set up very similar to the builder's side". Houzz Pro drafts the weekly client update from the week's activity, and the pro edits and publishes it. Our Client tab mirrors the portal.
