What the findings have in common

Most of them are one of eight shapes. Fixing the shape — once, as a house pattern — fixes the findings under it and keeps new screens from repeating them.

Where I’d start

  1. The three blockers — L1 a parked lead jumps to Won, S1 an internal note prints on the client’s quote, T1 attachments fail to save. Two are small; all three are bugs, not design calls.
  2. One hand-off pattern — when a quote or contract changes state, offer the lead’s next stage right there: Q1 C1 C2 L2. The pipeline, Maison AI’s brief and Convert to Project then stay true without anyone noticing.
  3. “What needs me” on every list — to-do chips and a personal badge: L3 Q4 Q5 C5 T4 T5 V3.
  4. Buttons that say what they do — Q2 Q3 L6 V6: the ones a studio meets every day.
  5. The client’s phone — Q6 C3, and V2, the gap the new Visualizer welcome opened.

The use cases that were walked

Each audit played a studio member (and, on the client pages, the homeowner) doing real jobs end to end. Where each walk stalled, and what already works well.

How it ran: six parallel walkthroughs on a production build, each on its own scratch copy of the demo data (nothing touched the prototype’s own database), at 1440×1024 and on touch at 390×844 / 768×1024. Each finding was checked against the design register; where today’s behaviour was a deliberate decision, the card names it under “Decided before” rather than reversing it. The two blockers were confirmed in the code.
